| **What works** | **A run is a descent, and it survives the app closing.** Boot → Menu → `Descend` (or `Continue`) → a run that arrives in an arena, seals it, spends a threat budget on telegraphed waves, opens a door when they are dead, and puts the player one stage deeper with the budget, curves and scaling that go with it. **Core owns every outcome**; Unity reports facts and senses and renders events. Three archetypes fight differently — the Husk closes, the Spitter kites to 14 m and will not shoot through a pillar, the Bloater walks in and detonates — and all three are authored data on one shared body, told apart by tint and scale. Arenas change at a boundary, drawn from the mode's roster by seed and stage. Everything the player cannot see has an indicator: screen-edge arrows for off-frame threats, a dimmer marker for a focus held out of range, rings for spawns and blasts. A run writes itself to disk at its opening frame and at every boundary, and deletes itself on death. **And as of M3-01a a kill is worth something:** experience is banked on the death, drained on the tick between the death check and the director, and crossing a threshold publishes a level and banks a pick — visible on the debug overlay as `lvl 3 0.42 owed 2`. **As of M3-01b the level is in the file**: the boundary write carries level, absolute XP and banked picks, a resumed run comes back at the level it had before `RunStarted` is published, and **a `run.json` written by the M2 build still loads** — it migrates to v2 and means what v1 meant, level 1 and nothing earned. **And as of M3-03 a run has a live tree**: given a class with one, it knows which nodes may be taken, taking one puts its effects on the player's stats through the registry, a resumed run gets its picks back *before* its hit points are clamped against the maximum those picks moved, and the boundary write carries them in take order. **As of M3-04 the game can also say which three of those a player would be shown** — a seeded, weighted, no-replacement draw from the available set, one draw per pick, from the `Offers` stream and no other, so the same seed against the same tree state always gives the same offer in the same order. Nothing calls it yet. **And as of M3-06 the game can act without the player being asked**: an owned Active has a live cooldown with CH §4.1's 40 % floor under it, an authored condition read off the `CombatBlackboard` every tick, and a cast that applies its effects and publishes `SkillCast` — at most one a tick, in take order, allocating nothing. The runner ticks immediately after combat and **above** the projectile step, so a trigger reading `IncomingProjectiles` sees the bolts still in the air rather than the ones that just landed. Nothing owns an Active in any shipped build, so the overlay reads `actives 0` — which is the runner correctly empty and saying so. The Charge obeys the same floor, invisibly, until M3-12 gives it something to obey. **And as of M3-07a the player can take that decision back:** every owned Active carries CC §6.1's switch, Auto by default, and a skill moved to Manual stops auto-casting *and* stops having its condition evaluated at all — it takes one of four thumb slots, keeps whatever cooldown it was in the middle of, and fires only when `CastSkill(slot)` arrives. The fifth is refused loudly so M3-09's screen has to ask CC §6.2's question instead of silently swapping. Nothing owns an Active and no screen sends either command, so the overlay reads `slots 0/4` beside `actives 0` — both correctly empty and saying so. **And as of M3-08a a pick can finally be spent, and the game can stop:** a run that is owed a pick and has a tree to spend it on opens a level-up **on the frame after the tick that earned it**, draws up to three nodes from the `Offers` stream, and stops being ticked at all until something chooses — `Time.timeScale` 0 and 30 fps with it, so animations and telegraphs freeze with the simulation rather than carrying on over it. Choosing takes the node, spends the pick, hands a new Active straight to the runner, and re-draws for the next pick over the **new** tree state. A pick with nothing left to offer is **Overflow** instead: +2 % damage and +2 % max HP per level, pooled additively under one source, granted silently and announced rather than shown. **A gated frame costs zero simulated seconds**, so `RunState.Time` is now play time and a stopwatch is play + screens. Nothing in the build ships a tree, so every run still banks its levels and nothing pauses — which is rule 5 working, not a gap. **And as of M3-09a the run can be stopped on purpose, which is the first thing this milestone has built that a player of the *current* build can actually reach** — every screen before it needs a tree and nothing in `Data/Trees` ships yet. ## How to write an entry

Append one entry per merged task, newest at the bottom. **Twelve lines or fewer.** The spec's *As built* footer owns the full account of what deviated and why; this entry is the index — what happened, and where each lesson was filed. (The archived logs average 7 KB an entry against a template that said "one line"; the cap is what keeps this file readable.)

```markdown
### YYYY-MM-DD · M0-03 · Domain events
**Built:** one or two lines — what exists now that didn't.
**Deviations:** none | *n*, in the spec's As built. Name here only one that changes a decision.
**Learned:** one line each, ending with where it was filed — Traps §n · AR §18.x · ledger row n · spent (stays here).
**Follow-ups:** tasks created, ledger rows added, or "none".
```

The PR number lives in the merge commit — do not write it here. Current State is written **as of the PR's merge**: the task the PR closes is *Last merged*. If a deviation changes a decision in `Architecture.md`, it needs a superseding ADR — say so here and link it.

**A durable lesson does not go in Current State.** A toolchain trap goes in [Traps.md](../Traps.md); a rule the code now depends on goes in [Architecture.md §18](../Architecture.md#18-invariants); a thing a named task must handle goes in the [carry-forward ledger](ROADMAP.md#carry-forward-into-m3), and one with no owner yet in the ROADMAP parking lot, one line. The entry says *what happened*, and links.

**At the end of a milestone**, move its entries to `archive/PROGRESS-M<n>.md`, promote anything durable out of them first, and leave this Log holding only the milestone in progress. **M2 is the first milestone this was done for on time** — M0's and M1's were archived a milestone late and at 381 KB between them, which is the failure the rule was written against.
